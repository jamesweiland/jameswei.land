## My Background
I've liked math & physics since I was young, but I never knew what direction I wanted to go. My senior year of high school, I was fortunate enough to have a great AP Physics teacher who had a PhD in Mechanical Engineering. I liked him, so I decided to follow his footsteps.

I never coded before college, other than dragging blocks around in Scratch.
My freshman semester, I took CS101 (an intro to Python course for MechE students), and I hated it! For a while, this course reinforced my belief that I just wasn't a programmer.

I got my first real taste of the cool things computers could do when I decided
to copy a Fireship video to run an uncensored variety of Mixtral on my laptop.
I had very little understanding of how LLMs worked back then, and I barely knew what a GPU was; 
it would take about 5 minutes to generate a single token.

I remember feeling like a real hacker using WSL!

At my internship the following summer, I found myself naturally writing Python scripts to automate tasks, scrape websites,
build predictive models, etc. This was my moment of realization that I wanted to
build software. I spent my free time that summer teaching myself Java, so I would have a head
start on a CS minor the following semester. Eventually, the majority of my free time was spent
working on random projects. This compounded into getting a job as a software engineer after graduation.
